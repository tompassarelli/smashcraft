# Single-player modes

Which single-player modes fighting-game players value, which of Smashcraft's
candidates to build first, and why. Issue
[#285](https://github.com/tompassarelli/smashcraft/issues/285); Classic is
[#284](https://github.com/tompassarelli/smashcraft/issues/284), the frame
story and adventure are
[#269](https://github.com/tompassarelli/smashcraft/issues/269). Researched
8 Oct 2026.

Statements marked **Judgement** are reasoning, not findings. Everything else
cites a source listed at the end with its date.

## How the evidence was gathered

- **Reach.** Steam's global achievement page shows the share of a game's
  Steam players who unlocked each achievement. Where an achievement is tied to
  a mode (finish the tutorial, finish Arcade once, finish the story), that
  share is a measured lower bound on how many players used the mode. All
  figures below were read on 8 Oct 2026. Console players are not counted,
  and Smash has no Steam release, so Smash evidence comes from reviews,
  developer statements and what Nintendo patched in after launch.
- **Expectation.** A mode is *expected* when its absence was punished:
  reviewers or players named it as missing, or the developer added it after
  launch.
- **Delight.** A mode *delights* when reviews single it out as the best part
  of the package, or the developer reports it as one of the most played.
- No representative player survey of mode usage was found. The only poll is
  a self-selected ResetEra thread (208 votes: 62% prefer Story, 33% Arcade).

What the table shows:

1. **The learning modes reach the most players.** Nearly every SF6 player
   opened the tutorials and most opened training; three in four Strive
   players finished its tutorial. No other single-player mode comes close.
2. **A short ladder with an ending is finished by about one player in
   three**, and the figure is the same in three unrelated games (SF6 35.5%,
   Tekken 8 31.4%, Strive 34.2%). That is close to, or above, the share who
   played a modest amount online.
3. **A cinematic story that alternates scenes with fights is finished about
   as often as Arcade** (MK1 35.1%, Tekken 8 28.3%), but **long RPG,
   avatar and episode modes are finished by fewer than one in ten** (SF6
   World Tour 6.5%, MK1 Invasions 7.7%) even when most players start them
   (World Tour prologue 57.9%).
4. **Side minigames reach about one player in ten** in games outside Smash.

### Expected: what players punish a game for missing

- **Arcade and tutorials.** Street Fighter V launched in Feb 2016 without an
  arcade mode or tutorials; reviewers called it "a severely lacklustre single
  player game" and its story chapters "fleeting and inconsequential" (Game
  Informer, Feb 2016). Capcom's Yoshinori Ono: "we underestimated the
  popularity of some of the single-player features" (MCV, 7 Apr 2016).
  Capcom added Arcade in 2018. Virtua Fighter 5 R.E.V.O. (2025) was called
  "bare next to modern fighters" for shipping only Arcade and Training;
  reviewers treat its World Stage update (Nov 2025) as the fix. Invincible VS (2026) is faulted for "not
  a lot of single-player options".
- **A story for the cast.** Rivals of Aether II's missing story mode is named
  as "a significant loss compared to the original"; the developer has put it
  on the roadmap for 2028. Smash 4 reviewers noted "no substantial
  single-player story mode" (Giant Bomb, Nov 2014).
- **Smash's score-attack modes, for Smash players.** Ultimate shipped without
  Home-Run Contest, Target Test and the Events; fans named them ("the good
  modes are all gone (All-Star/Home Run/Target/etc)") and Nintendo added
  Home-Run Contest in update 5.0 (Sept 2019). Sakurai has said Break the
  Targets was built as an indirect tutorial and that dropping a real tutorial
  from Ultimate is a regret (Source Gaming, Jan 2020).
- **The casual majority is the audience.** Harada: "the fan base is about
  70% casual ... most of the players will never go to a tournament"
  (PlayStation Blog, June 2017); in 2021 he put casual players at about 85%
  (EventHubs, Apr 2021).
  Riot is ending active development of 2XKO, which shipped with no story or
  arcade, in Dec 2026 because players did not stay beyond its core
  fighting-game audience (Riot, 20 Aug 2026). **Judgement:** Riot gave no
  mode-level cause, so this is consistent with the pattern rather than proof
  of it.

### Delight: what drives praise

- **A themed route per fighter.** Ultimate's Classic is "the most enjoyable
  and creative Classic Mode yet" because "each character seemingly has a list
  of opponents and stages that are themed around themselves" (Well Played,
  Dec 2018). Classic is "where Smash veterans will feel the most at home".
- **Scripted fights that recreate a character.** Ultimate's spirit battles:
  "each spirit fight is creative and brilliant, and seeing how each spirit is
  represented in its battle stays interesting" (Well Played, Dec 2018);
  Gamereactor praises a fight on a windy stage against a possessed golden
  Squirtle. Smash 4's Events grid was "a fun replacement" for a story (Giant
  Bomb, Nov 2014). Melee's Events pay homage to each character's history.
- **A cinematic story with fights in it.** NetherRealm: "All the data bears
  out the fact that our single player mode is one of our most popular ways
  for people to access and experience the game" (Cianciolo, 2019). MK1's
  story is "predictably great" even where critics found it familiar (Sept
  2023). Marvel Tōkon's tournament-framed Episode Mode is "far more robust
  than initially expected" (Aug 2026).
- **Learning that feels like play.** Strive's Missions are its most
  consistent praise, "an invaluable resource for beginners" (NME, June 2021).
  Tekken 8's Arcade Quest is "a brilliant place to start for newcomers"
  (TrueAchievements, Jan 2024).

### What disappoints

- **Length without variety.** World of Light is "long and repetitive ...
  still just a continuous series of fights" (Tired Old Hack, Dec 2018); MK1's
  Invasions is the most frequent complaint, "a grind for cosmetics" (Sept
  2023); Fatal Fury's Episodes of South Town is "repetitive" (2025).
- **Mode novelty that isn't fighting.** Smash Tour was "half-hearted" (Giant
  Bomb, Nov 2014). SF6's World Tour splits players: "a ridiculous,
  single-player delight" (Filmstories, 2023) against "long and boring" (The
  Outer Haven, 2023), and 6.5% finish it.
- **A story with no fights.** Strive's story is a film you watch; GameGeeker
  scores it 6.5/10 and says it "will either delight you or leave you cold"
  (2021).
- **Thin per-fighter stories.** SF6's Arcade stories "won't win any awards"
  (Tom's Guide, 2023); Windows Central saw no reason to finish it "with more
  than just a handful of fighters" (2023). Players finish it once, not 21
  times: Rivals II's best Hard Arcade clear is 3.7%.

### Expected versus delight

| Mode | Expected (missing is punished) | Delights (drives praise) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training | Yes, everywhere | Rarely praised on its own |
| Basic tutorial | Yes (SFV, Ultimate regret, highest reach) | When it plays like a mode (Missions, Arcade Quest) |
| Arcade / Classic ladder | Yes, everywhere | When each fighter's route is themed (Ultimate) |
| Cinematic story with fights | Yes for MK, Tekken and Rivals audiences | Yes, when short and fight-led |
| Scripted event matches | Smash players notice (Events, spirits) | Yes: the creativity of each recreation |
| Score-attack minigames | Smash players only (Home-Run patched in) | Speedrun and nostalgia niche |
| Long adventure or RPG mode | Smash players remember Subspace | Splits players; under 10% finish |

## Smashcraft's candidates

What Smashcraft has to reuse (main, 8 Oct 2026): 21 fighters, a home stage
for each (smashcraft:docs/design/home-stages.md), six named computer
opponents with five difficulty tiers each
(smashcraft:docs/design/cpu-profiles.md), Stocks, Time and Endless rules
(smashcraft:docs/design/match-flow.md), deterministic hazards, and Training
with partner behaviours, frame readout and combo counter
(smashcraft:docs/design/training-mode.md, #120, shipped). Warcraft III itself
supplies in-engine cinematics: letterbox, unit portraits with speech
("transmissions"), camera paths.

Scores run 1 to 5. **Cost is scored so that 5 is cheapest.** Expectation
and payoff come from the evidence above; cost and Warcraft fit are
**Judgement**.

| Rank | Candidate | Expect. | Payoff | Cost | Warcraft fit | Total | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Classic: a themed route per fighter (#284) | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 16 | Expected in every game; one in three finish a ladder. Reuses fighters, home stages and the named opponents' tiers as the difficulty curve. New work: a route table for 21 fighters, route progression, a boss rule and an ending card. Warcraft's rivalries (Illidan and Maiev, Arthas and Illidan) write the routes. |
| 2 | Lore Battles: scripted matches recreating Warcraft moments | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 16 | The Events and spirit-battle pattern, which drives praise for creativity. Each battle is a data entry over existing pieces: fighters, stage, opponent tier, stocks, time, starting damage, a win condition and a one-line transmission. Warcraft III's campaign is a list of named moments the audience knows. |
| 3 | Basic tutorial | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 15 | The highest-reach single-player mode in every game measured, and its absence was punished (SFV) and regretted (Ultimate). Runs inside Training with the existing partner; a lesson passes on an action the sim already counts. Players arriving from Warcraft III custom maps know neither platform-fighter controls nor Smash's rules. |
| 4 | Tournament frame cinematic (#269) | 2 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 13 | Alone it is a film, and story without fights splits players (Strive). As a 30-60 second opening for Classic it is cheap with Warcraft's own cinematic tools and gives the routes their reason. Tekken and Marvel Tōkon use the same tournament frame. |
| 5 | Home-Run Contest | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 11 | Smash players noticed its absence and Nintendo patched it in. Needs a sandbag object and a distance readout. A polymorphed sheep as the sandbag fits Warcraft's humour. |
| 6 | Multi-Man (waves of computer fighters) | 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 11 | Present in every Smash; outside Smash wave modes reach about 10%. Reuses computer players; cost depends on how many fighters a match can hold at once, which this research did not check. Waves of Footmen or Ghouls fit Warcraft. |
| 7 | Adventure campaign (#269) | 2 | 4 | 1 | 5 | 12 | Highest Warcraft fit and highest ceiling, but the costliest by far (new level and unit-against-hero systems), and the evidence on long modes is poor: World Tour 6.5% finished, World of Light "long and repetitive", Subspace remembered fondly but dropped by its own director because a large cast made a coherent story impossible and uploaded footage spoiled it. |
| 8 | Per-fighter combo trials | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 9 | A quarter to two fifths of players try a few trials. Authoring and checking trials for 21 fighters is per-fighter work that changes with every balance pass. |
| 9 | Target Test | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 9 | Smash nostalgia (Melee's per-fighter stages are a speedrun scene), but per-fighter stages are expensive; Brawl's shared levels were the cheap version and were less loved. |

Training is not ranked: it is expected everywhere and already shipped.

## MVP set (proposed; Tom approves before any build issue opens)

1. **Classic with the tournament opening.** Each of the 21 fighters gets a
   route of about six one-stock or two-stock fights on themed stages: lore
   rivals, their home stage, then a lore boss. Difficulty rises through the
   named opponents' tiers from the player's chosen starting tier. The boss is
   the cheapest version that reads as a boss, such as a roster fighter scaled
   up with extra stocks (**Judgement**; #284 settles the boss rules). Each
   route ends with an ending card: portrait, two or three lines in
   Warcraft's transmission style, and a results line with time and damage
   taken. The first time Classic starts, a 30-60 second in-engine opening
   sets the frame: the heroes put their wars aside for a tournament, serious
   but a little goofy (#269). Not required: per-fighter cutscenes, voice
   acting, unlocks, a bonus stage.

2. **Lore Battles.** Ten to twelve scripted matches, each recreating one
   Warcraft III moment with existing pieces, for example Lich King against
   Illidan on Frozen Throne, Forsaken Paladin against Dreadlord at
   Stratholme, Warden hunting Illidan in the Tomb of Sargeras, Illidan
   against Pit Lord at Hellfire Citadel, Archer holding Nordrassil. Each is
   one data entry: the player's fighter, the opponent and tier, the stage,
   rule changes from existing settings (stocks, time limit, starting damage),
   one win condition from a small set (KO, survive the clock, win within the
   clock) and a one-line transmission before the fight. A list shows each
   battle and whether it has been cleared this session. Not required: new
   mechanics, new stages, cutscenes, saved progress across sessions.

3. **Basic tutorial.** Six to eight lessons on the Training stage with the
   existing partner: move and dash, jump and double jump, attacks and
   specials, shield and dodge, grab and throw, recover to the ledge, and
   KO the partner. Each lesson shows one instruction and passes when the
   player performs the action a set number of times, counted by the sim.
   Any lesson can be replayed, and a new player is offered the tutorial
   before their first match. Not required: per-fighter lessons, combo
   trials, a narrated character guide.

**Judgement:** Classic and Lore Battles share one engine, a list of
configured matches, so building Lore Battles second costs mostly data. The
same list of configured matches on a map is the core of World of Light, so
both also prepare the adventure.

## Order for the rest

4. Home-Run Contest (sheep): the cheapest Smash staple, expected by the
   platform-fighter audience.
5. Multi-Man: reuses computer players; check how many fighters a match can
   hold first.
6. Per-fighter combo trials: after the roster's balance settles, so trials
   are not rewritten every pass.
7. Target Test: shared stages first, per-fighter only if the shared ones are
   played.
8. Adventure campaign (#269): built from Lore Battles' configured matches on
   a Warcraft map, once Classic and Lore Battles show how much players play.
